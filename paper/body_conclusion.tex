\section{Conclusion}

\textbf{WhiteTiger} is a billion-frame, fully human-teleoperated, multi-platform robot manipulation dataset for embodied foundation model adaptation and event-aware robot learning. The dataset contains \textbf{12877.59 hours} of robot data, \textbf{6195 tasks}, \textbf{737575 episodes}, and \textbf{1390781176 frames} across \textbf{14 robot platforms}. Platform-specific HDF5 operation records are converted into LeRobot v2.1, providing a standardized training interface while preserving embodiment-specific observation, state, action, and camera schemas.

WhiteTiger couples physical demonstration scale with temporal structure. Dense trajectories support foundation-model adaptation under real robot kinematics, sensing layouts, contact dynamics, and end-effector constraints. Event- and keyframe-level annotations expose task progress and semantic milestones beyond gripper or dexterous-hand state changes, including alignment, boundary crossing, handover, deformation, and object-state transitions. This structure enables research on event-conditioned imitation, hierarchical skill decomposition, progress-aware value learning, future-state prediction, and phase-aware evaluation.

We plan to conduct an offline open-loop zero-shot evaluation on external datasets excluded from WhiteTiger training. The planned protocol will compare a pretrained foundation-model baseline with a WhiteTiger-fine-tuned model on identical held-out trajectories and will report normalized full-action and joint-only prediction errors. The current version does not report results from this evaluation and makes no quantitative claim about offline zero-shot improvement.

Future work will also measure data-scaling behavior, held-out-platform transfer, additional policy families, closed-loop execution, and the effect of event-derived supervision on real-robot success. These evaluations are required to isolate the dataset factors that govern policy scaling and generalization in open-world manipulation.
